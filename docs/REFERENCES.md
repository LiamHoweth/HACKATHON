### How Still Here differs (our implemented combination)

This describes only what our repo implements. It is not a claim that the combination is new. Much of it is not yet iPad-verified, and some parts were uncommitted at the time of writing (see the README feature table). Still Here is a web app for iPad Safari. All camera processing happens on the device: MediaPipe Face Landmarker blendshapes detect two facial gestures, not gaze. Holding both eyebrows up for 500 ms (**NEXT**) moves the highlight forward exactly one option. A calibrated, deliberate long blink (**SELECT**) chooses the option highlighted when the eyes closed. A gesture arbiter makes sure one physical cycle produces one command. The model offers three different replies (OpenAI Responses API, strict `json_schema`, `store: false`), along with "None of these", guidance, quick phrases, and a scanning keyboard. The chosen sentence opens on a review screen that starts on **Cancel**. Speaking it takes a separate, new blink. Only that confirmed sentence is sent, from our server, to ElevenLabs. The voice is a prebuilt one, not a clone. Learning is limited to length preferences the user consents to. Auto-scan, keyboard or switch, and touch remain as fallbacks, and all of them go through the same review-and-confirm path. VisioALS (gaze quadrants, a cloned ElevenLabs voice) and DEAN (partner ASR, user-selected replies) overlap heavily with parts of this pipeline.

Each card says "Licensed under Apache License, Version 2.0" | Present locally |

**Do the model terms allow our use?** The license (Apache-2.0) allows use, modification, and redistribution, provided the license notice is kept. The model cards also say what the models are meant for, which is separate from the license. The Face Mesh V2 and Blendshape V2 cards name **AR entertainment** as the primary intended application. Their out-of-scope list includes "This model is not intended for human life-critical decisions." They also say the outputs do not provide facial recognition or identification. Our use as an AAC input sits **outside the stated primary domain**. A missed or false selection in communication matters to the user, though the confirm step keeps the model from deciding anything on its own. This is not a license barrier. Still, it is a reason to describe the gestures as prototype estimates, keep the review/Cancel/confirm step and the non-camera fallbacks, and avoid any reliability claim.

The eye-closure and brow-raise signals come from the model's blendshape outputs (`eyeBlinkLeft`/`eyeBlinkRight`; `browInnerUp`, `browOuterUpLeft`, `browOuterUpRight`). These are facial-gesture estimates. They are not gaze, identity, or emotion, and we have no evidence that they are clinically validated. The Face Mesh V2 card reports landmark-error fairness results across 17 geographic subregions, perceived gender, and skin tone. Those cover landmark accuracy only, not blink or brow-raise blendshape accuracy, and not people with motor impairments, glasses, or difficult lighting. We have not verified the models' behavior for our users. That needs our own testing.
