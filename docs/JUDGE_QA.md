# Judge Q&A

These answers are grounded in the evidence as of 12:10 CT. **Reconcile them with [TEST_RESULTS.md](TEST_RESULTS.md) and [PRESENTATION_LOG.md](PRESENTATION_LOG.md) just before judging.** Replace "not measured" or "not verified" only with recorded results.

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| **How reliable is the eyebrow detection?** | **Not measured.** *(After the iPad trial, give the counts from TEST_RESULTS: raises attempted, committed NEXTs, missed NEXTs, inadvertent NEXTs during 2 minutes of normal movement, plus lighting, glasses, and tester.)* It's a small team test, not a reliability study, and it says nothing about people with ALS. |
| **Why eyebrows for NEXT instead of waiting on auto-scan?** | With auto-scan, you wait for the highlight to come around. With an eyebrow raise, you move when you're ready, one step per raise. Auto-scan + blink is still there for anyone who finds raises hard. That's a design argument. We have not measured whether it's faster. |
| **Why half a second? How is it calibrated?** | 500 ms was the user's requested target: long enough to filter brief brow movements during speech, short enough to feel responsive. Calibration samples neutral brows and a few comfortable raises to set the thresholds per person. It's measured with a monotonic clock, not a frame count. Holding longer still moves only one step. A raise must relax to neutral before the next one counts. The thresholds are prototype values, not clinical ones. |
| **How reliable is the blink selection?** | **Not measured on a real camera.** Synthetic tests pass. Short natural blinks fall under the calibrated minimum closure and are ignored. Both eyes must close, so winks don't count. |
| **Can a gesture accidentally make it speak?** | Speaking needs a selection, then a review that starts on Cancel, an eyebrow raise to Speak, and a **new** long blink, from a different input cycle, at least 600 ms after review opens. One gesture can't both select and confirm, and a raise can't move the highlight once a blink has started. Unit tests cover the kernel side. **The gesture side has not been tested on an iPad.** Someone can still deliberately confirm the wrong message, which is why review shows the exact text. |
| **What if someone can't raise their eyebrows or blink reliably?** | Then they shouldn't have to. Calibration fails openly, and auto-scan + blink, keyboard/switch, and touch reach every screen through the same guarded path. |
| **Is the voice natural? Is it cloned?** | We use ElevenLabs (`eleven_v4_turbo` is the preferred model) with a prebuilt voice, not a clone of anyone. **As of 12:05 the key isn't configured, so we haven't heard it in our app yet.** *(After TTS-09, describe the five listening lines and what listeners noticed, with the listener count. No naturalness score unless listeners gave one.)* |
| **How does Safari play audio after a blink? Doesn't iPad block autoplay?** | It may. iPad Safari can block audio that a real touch didn't start, and a camera gesture may not count. So setup has a one-time **Enable Audio / Test Voice** touch, possibly by a helper. Whether later blink-confirmed speech plays without another touch is **not iPad-verified** yet. If Safari blocks it, the app shows "Audio blocked" instead of pretending it spoke. |
| **What goes to ElevenLabs?** | Only the exact sentence the person confirmed, sent from our server. No suggestions, no history, no camera data. Nothing is synthesized in advance. The app doesn't store the audio. ElevenLabs' retention depends on the account plan, which we haven't reviewed, so we don't claim zero retention. |
| **What happens if ElevenLabs fails?** | The app shows the exact text and offers a clearly labeled **basic device voice**. Because the first attempt may have partly played, it needs a fresh review and confirmation. It never switches silently. |
| **Is the camera recording me?** | No recording is designed in. The small top-right preview is a live view only. Frames go to a face-landmark model running in Safari, loaded from our own server, which gives brow and eye-closure scores and a face count. *(Say "and the network log showed no frames uploaded" only after SYS-13 is recorded.)* No face recognition, gaze tracking, or emotion detection. The camera stays off until you consent. |
| **What if someone else walks into the frame, or I look away?** | Losing the face cancels any gesture in progress and pauses. Coming back selects nothing by itself; a deliberate Resume is needed. With two faces, nothing can be selected. That's in code and synthetic tests. Face count is not identity. |
| **Has this been done before?** | Yes. AI-assisted AAC and camera-based facial-gesture access both exist. VisioALS, Bobbai, Speak4Me, Google's SpeakFaster, and Project Converse are research leads in our references. We haven't independently verified them. We are not claiming a first. |
| **What is your contribution?** | A specific working combination built today: eyebrow NEXT and long-blink SELECT on an iPad, semantic "None of these" scoped to one question, explicit intent guidance, conversation continuity, consent-gated preferences, and a natural voice that speaks only the confirmed sentence, all through one guarded engine. *(List only items verified in the presentation log.)* |
| **Why AI rather than predictive typing?** | The hypothesis is that choosing a whole message takes fewer gestures than spelling it. We **have not measured** that. My words, a scanning keyboard, is always available. |
| **What happens when the AI is wrong?** | Reject all three, guide the direction, ask for "same idea, shorter", or write your own. After two rejected batches the app offers "Write it myself". |
| **How much does it cost?** | OpenAI per reply batch and ElevenLabs per spoken character. Paid routes sit behind an access code, and the speech route has a server-side daily character budget (in the uncommitted code at 12:10; confirm it's deployed). We haven't measured cost per conversation. |
| **What happens without internet?** | Gesture detection, Quick phrases, and My words don't need the AI. The natural voice needs the network. Offline behavior after a network drop is **not verified**. |
| **How does it learn?** | Only with session adaptation on, and only sentence length. After 3 different turns it may offer a suggestion. Nothing is saved without "Yes, remember this" and a separate confirmation. |
| **Can someone with ALS use it?** | We don't know. Access needs vary. **No one with ALS or any AAC user has tested it.** |
| **What did you measure?** | *(From TEST_RESULTS only.)* As of 12:05: nothing. Live AI returned valid batches in about 4 s in single synthetic calls. That's an observation, not a latency study. |
| **Did you use live AI in the demo?** | *(Say what the badge shows.)* Live OpenAI `gpt-6-astra` was verified today with synthetic inputs. The fixture mode is labeled. |
| **Is there a native app?** | An experimental SwiftUI client exists in the repo, but it's not part of the demo. The demo is the web app in iPad Safari. |
| **How was AI used to build this?** | Claude Code implemented it under Liam's direction and review. See [AI_USAGE.md](AI_USAGE.md). |
| **What would clinical deployment require?** | Evaluation and co-design with AAC users and specialists, measured reliability across people and conditions, accessibility review, a privacy and retention review of each provider, and regulatory advice. None of that has been done. |
